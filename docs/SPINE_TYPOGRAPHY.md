# Spine typography

Every Apple TV view that draws a spine letters it by the iPhone and Mac app's rule (`SpineLettering` in `ios/Bookworms/Views/SpineLettering.swift`), with the Apple TV's minimum sizes: the shelf and Ambient through `SpineView`, and Book Wall through its spine textures. `SpineTextMetrics` in [SpineTextMetrics.swift](../Bookworms/Services/SpineTextMetrics.swift) measures the rule with UIKit (`SpineTextMetrics.measure`), so a spine of a given frame and face letters the same in every view, up to scale. Keep the shares, the title's line rule (`twoLineGain`, `oneLineFloor`, `twoLineFill`, `lineGap`), and the author forms the same in both apps; each copy names the other.

The spine reads top to bottom: the title at the head, the author at the tail, both centered across the spine. A book with a format reserves the format icon's room at the tail (`formatIconRoom(for:length:thickness:)`) before lettering the rest of the length: the 18-point icon and its margin from the tail end. The margin is 20 points (`formatIconMargin`); on a spine with [blind-stamped bands](BOOK_WALL.md#flat-cloth-spine), it grows so the icon clears the tail band's nearer line by 12 points (`formatIconBandGap`), and the lettering's room shrinks with it. The icon (`formatIconCenter(for:length:thickness:)`) stands upright on the standing spine; it doesn't turn with the lettering. It draws in hierarchical rendering, so the Ebook symbol's screen has a faint fill on every spine. In that lettered length:

- Each end keeps a margin of 3.5% of the length plus 25% of the thickness. The title and author share a box 88% of the thickness tall, 30% of the thickness apart.
- The title stops at its first colon; the shelf spine draws no subtitle. It starts at the box's size, with no other cap, and shrinks on a half-point grid to the largest size that fits. Lines break only between words, at the break whose widest line is narrowest, so no word splits and a title without spaces keeps to one line.
- The title keeps to one line unless two lines, sized by their line boxes, letter it at least 25% larger (`twoLineGain`). Where one line would letter below 4.5% of the lettered length (`oneLineFloor`), it takes two lines whenever they are larger at all.
- A title on two lines then grows until its letters fill 85% of the box (`twoLineFill`), or its longer line fills the title's width. The letters' height is measured from the title's own glyphs: a font's line box leaves room for the tallest and lowest letters of any text, which a title in capitals doesn't use. The lines sit a fifth of the letters' height apart (`lineGap`), and the letters, not their line boxes, are centered across the spine. `SpineLettering.titlePitch` and `titleShift` carry that spacing to the views that draw the spine. A title whose letters already fill their line boxes keeps its line height.
- The Apple TV's safeguard: where those rules letter the title below 23 points, it takes whichever of one line and two filled lines is larger. It takes a third line, filled the same way, only when that is larger still, at the spine's final dimensions, after `readableDimensions` has grown it.
- The title first claims the width its one line needs at the box's size. The author gets the rest: at least 24% of the length, or its surname's width at the minimum size if that is wider, at most 40%, and no more than its form needs. The title takes back what the author leaves.
- The author lists the first name and "et al." for more than two names, or for two names longer than 32 characters. It takes the first form that fits: the whole name on one line, then on two lines (two names one over the other, or one name broken before its surname), at its large size where that applies, then at its small size. Only a name that fits neither way at either size takes the surname alone, at the same sizes. Its large size, half the thickness, applies only where the title keeps its line count, loses no size to it, and stays at least 1.4 times its size; otherwise it letters at a little over a quarter of the thickness. Both sizes stop at 4% of the length and never fall below 16 points. The author sits at the tail-end margin, and a two-line name's lines are centered on each other.
- The title stays at least 4 points larger than the author: the author drops to the title's size less 4 points, but not below 16 points. A surname too long for the author's widest room truncates with an ellipsis; no other form truncates.

Titles target at least 23 points. When a title on one or two lines sized by their line boxes would letter smaller (`growthTitleSize`), `readableDimensions` lengthens the spine within the shelf's height and the book-shape bounds, then thickens it. Growth leaves out a two-line title's fill on purpose: a spine that counted on the fill would stop growing sooner, so the fill would shrink the book in place of enlarging its letters, and would take the room its author's whole name needs. A spine that growth brought to 23 points draws its title at 23 points or more, though the preference for one line can draw it smaller than the size growth measured. Page packing uses the adjusted dimensions. Unusually long unbreakable text can still fall below 23 points in the bounded layout.

Lettering is measured once, when shelf pages are prepared (`BookPresentation.pages`), and carried in `ShelfPage.letterings`. `SpineView` draws each measured line at its measured size, face, and pitch, without rewrapping or scaling, so focus changes and view updates never measure text. Book Wall measures its lettering off the main actor while it prepares the wall (`BookWallRasterizer.layout`), at the wall's viewing scale on the Apple TV screen, carries it in each `BookWallLayout.Slot`, and draws it into the spine texture in the same arrangement; see [Book Wall genre and typeface](BOOK_WALL.md#genre-and-typeface). A face registered after preparation draws once the pages are prepared again for the new font revision; until then the spine draws the face it was measured in. Letterings and per-book dimensions use bounded caches keyed by text or book, resolved face, and geometry. `ShelfView` keeps its prepared pages keyed by content revision, font revision, and geometry.

Ambient mode is an exception: it calls `BookPresentation.pages` from its view body. The caches limit repeated measurement, but a cold cache or a newly registered face measures during a view update. Ambient draws covers only while `BookPresentation.showsGeneratedSpines` is `false`, so it measures no lettering today. Prepare Ambient's pages outside the body before turning generated spines on.

UIKit supplies fonts at their rendered point size, retaining native optical sizing when a custom font supports it. The fitter confirms each size by measuring at that size.

## Shelf controls and focus

Shelf focus, paging, and returns follow [focus and remote navigation](FOCUS_NAVIGATION.md). Settings and Ambient are sidebar items; the shelf has no controls below it.

The physical paging test accepts the current page boundary through `TEST_RUNNER_SHELF_LAST_BOOK_ID`, `TEST_RUNNER_SHELF_NEXT_BOOK_ID` in the xcodebuild environment. It starts a Debug build on page 2 using `--start-shelf-page=1`, uses the real library, checks both directions twice, verifies that backward focus persists, and checks that Right from the sidebar returns to the same book. Without those values it skips. The simulator navigation tests cover page transitions with sample data.
